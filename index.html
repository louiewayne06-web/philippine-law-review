Data Privacy Act of 2012

The Data Privacy Act of 2012 (R.A. 10173) is the Philippines' primary law protecting personal information. It establishes the National Privacy Commission and regulates how organizations process personal data.

Key Points:

  • Applies to all natural and juridical persons processing personal data
  • Protects fundamental right to privacy in communications
  • Covers personal information and sensitive personal information
  • Establishes rights of data subjects
  • Prescribes penalties for violations
Core Purpose: Protects the fundamental right to privacy by regulating the collection, recording, organization, storage, updating, retrieval, transmission, and erasure of personal information.
01
Personal Data Rights — Data subjects have 7 rights: to be informed, to object, to access, to rectification, to erasure/blocking, to damages, and data portability.
02
Lawful Bases for Processing — Processing must be based on: consent, contract fulfillment, legal obligation, vital interests, public order/safety, or legitimate interests.
03
Sensitive Personal Data — Extra protection for medical, health, genetic, biometric, religious, political, and union membership information.
04
Penalties — Violations range from 1-6 years imprisonment and ₱500k-₱2M fines depending on the offense severity.
Multiple Choice — Question 1
Which of the following is NOT a lawful basis for processing personal information under the DPA?
A
Data subject consent
B
Fulfilling a contract
C
Compliance with legal obligation
D
Simply because the organization feels like it
✅ Correct! Organizations cannot process personal data without a lawful basis.
