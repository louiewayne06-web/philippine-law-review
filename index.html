Data Privacy Act of 2012
Republic Act No. 10173 — An Act Protecting Individual Personal Information
Full Text
Amendments

✦ ✦ ✦
Section 1
Title

This Act shall be known as the "Data Privacy Act of 2012."

Section 2
Declaration of Policy

It is the policy of the State to protect the fundamental human right of privacy of communication while ensuring free flow of information to promote innovation and growth. The State recognizes the vital role of information and communications technology in nation-building and its inherent obligation to ensure that personal information in information and communications systems in the government and in the private sector are secured and protected.

Section 3
Definition of Terms

(a) Commission — refers to the National Privacy Commission created by virtue of this Act.

(b) Consent of the data subject — refers to any freely given, specific, informed indication of will, whereby the data subject agrees to the collection and processing of personal information about and/or relating to him or her.

(c) Data subject — refers to an individual whose personal information is processed.

(d) Filing — refers to the act of storing any personal information in a filing system.

(e) Information and Communications System — refers to a system for generating, sending, receiving, storing or otherwise processing electronic data messages or electronic documents.

(f) Personal information — refers to any information whether recorded in a material form or not, from which the identity of an individual is apparent or can be reasonably and directly ascertained.

(g) Personal information controller — refers to a natural or juridical person, or any other body who controls the processing of personal data.

(h) Personal information processor — refers to any natural or juridical person or any other body to whom a personal information controller may outsource or instruct the processing of personal data.

(i) Processing — refers to any operation or any set of operations performed upon personal information including, but not limited to, the collection, recording, organization, storage, updating or modification, retrieval, consultation, use, consolidation, blocking, erasure or destruction of data.

(j) Privileged information — refers to any and all forms of data which under the Rules of Court and other pertinent laws constitute privileged communication.

(k) Sensitive personal information — refers to personal information about an individual's race, ethnic origin, marital status, age, color, and religious, philosophical or political affiliations; about an individual's health, education, genetic or sexual life, or to proceedings for any offense committed or alleged.

Section 4
Scope of Application

This Act applies to the processing of all types of personal information and to any natural and juridical person involved in personal information processing including those personal information controllers and processors who, although not found or established in the Philippines, use equipment that are located in the Philippines, or those who maintain an office, branch or agency in the Philippines subject to the immediately succeeding paragraph.

Section 8
Lawful Processing of Personal Information

The processing of personal information shall be permitted only if not otherwise prohibited by law, and when at least one of the following conditions exists:

(a) The data subject has given his or her consent;

(b) The processing of personal information is necessary and is related to the fulfillment of a contract with the data subject;

(c) The processing is necessary for compliance with a legal obligation;

(d) The processing is necessary to protect vitally important interests of the data subject;

(e) The processing is necessary in order to respond to national emergency, to comply with the requirements of public order and safety, or to fulfill functions of public authority which necessarily includes the processing of personal data for the fulfillment of its mandate;

(f) The processing is necessary for the purposes of the legitimate interests pursued by the personal information controller or by a third party.

Section 16
Rights of the Data Subject

The data subject is entitled to:

(a) Right to be informed — whether personal information pertaining to him or her shall be, are being or have been processed.

(b) Right to object — to the processing of his or her personal information.

(c) Right to access — his or her personal information from the personal information controller.

(d) Right to rectification — of any error, inaccuracy, or incompleteness of his or her personal data.

(e) Right to erasure or blocking — his or her personal data from the personal information controller's filing system.

(f) Right to damages — from any Personal Information Controller or Processor who wrongfully collects, stores, uses, or otherwise processes their personal information.

(g) Right to data portability — to obtain from the personal information controller a copy of data undergoing processing in an electronic or structured format.

Sections 25–37
Penalties and Offenses

Unauthorized Processing (Sec. 25) — Imprisonment of 1–3 years and fine of ₱500,000–₱2,000,000 (general); 3–6 years and ₱500,000–₱4,000,000 (sensitive PI).

Accessing PI Due to Negligence (Sec. 26) — Imprisonment of 1–3 years and fine of ₱500,000–₱2,000,000.

Improper Disposal (Sec. 27) — Imprisonment of 6 months–2 years and fine of ₱100,000–₱500,000.

Unauthorized Purposes (Sec. 28) — Imprisonment of 1 year 6 months–5 years and fine of ₱500,000–₱1,000,000.

Unauthorized Disclosure (Sec. 31) — Imprisonment of 1–3 years (general); 3–5 years (sensitive PI) with corresponding fines.

Amendments and Related Issuances
IRR — 2016
Implementing Rules and Regulations (2016)

The National Privacy Commission issued the IRR on 24 August 2016 pursuant to Sec. 7(d) of R.A. 10173. The IRR provides the detailed procedures, definitions, and requirements for compliance with the Data Privacy Act, including the Data Protection Officer (DPO) designation requirement, registration requirements for personal information controllers, and breach notification procedures (within 72 hours of discovery of breach to NPC).

NPC Circular 16-01
Registration of Data Processing Systems

Requires personal information controllers and processors to register with the NPC before processing sensitive personal information or those involving at least 1,000 data subjects. Mandatory registration applies to government agencies, banks, and entities processing sensitive PI.

NPC Advisory No. 2017-01
Processing of Personal Data in the Context of Human Resources

Provides guidance on the lawful bases for processing employee personal data, including the scope of employee monitoring, CCTV use in workplaces, and the rights of employees as data subjects vis-à-vis legitimate employer interests.

NPC Advisory No. 2018-03
Data Sharing Agreements

Outlines the requirements for valid data sharing agreements between personal information controllers, including the need for a written agreement specifying purpose, duration, security measures, and the rights and obligations of the parties, as well as informing data subjects of such sharing.

Summary & Key Takeaways
R.A. 10173
Core Purpose: Protects the fundamental right to privacy by regulating the collection, recording, organization, storage, updating, modification, retrieval, consultation, use, consolidation, blocking, erasure, or destruction of personal data.
01
Three-Pronged Policy — Protect privacy, ensure free flow of information, and secure personal data in both government and private sectors.
02
National Privacy Commission (NPC) — Created as the independent oversight body to administer and enforce the DPA. Composed of a Privacy Commissioner and two Deputies serving 3-year terms.
03
Data Protection Principles (Sec. 11) — Personal data must be: (1) collected for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes; (2) processed fairly and lawfully; (3) accurate, relevant, and kept up to date; (4) adequate, not excessive; (5) kept for no longer than necessary.
04
Sensitive Personal Information — Receives higher protection. Includes race, religion, health, education, genetic/sexual life data, and criminal records. Processing requires explicit consent or specific legal basis.
05
Seven Rights of Data Subjects — To be informed, to object, to access, to rectification, to erasure/blocking, to damages, and to data portability.
06
Data Breach Notification — PICs must notify NPC and affected data subjects within 72 hours of discovery of a personal data breach likely to give rise to a real risk of serious harm.
07
Extraterritorial Application — Applies to processors using equipment located in the Philippines or maintaining an office/branch in the Philippines, regardless of where the controller is established.

E-Commerce Act of 2000
Republic Act No. 8792 — An Act Providing for the Recognition and Use of Electronic Commercial and Non-Commercial Transactions
Full Text
Amendments
Summary & Takeaways
Reviewer
Ask AI
✦ ✦ ✦
Section 1
Title

This Act shall be known as the "Electronic Commerce Act of 2000."

Section 2
Declaration of Policy

The State recognizes the vital role of information and communications technology (ICT) in nation-building; the need to create an information-friendly environment which supports and ensures the availability, diversity and affordability of ICT products and services; the primary responsibility of the private sector in contributing investments and services in telecommunications and information technology; the need to develop, with appropriate training programs and institutional policy changes, human resources for the information technology age, a labor force skilled in the use of ICT and a population capable of operating and utilizing electronic appliances and computers; its obligation to facilitate the transfer and promotion of adaptive technology, to ensure network security, connectivity, and neutrality of technology for the national benefit; and the need to marshal, organize and deploy national information infrastructures, comprising in particular of telecommunications network elements, to the service of the Filipino people.

Section 6
Legal Recognition of Data Messages

Information shall not be denied legal effect, validity or enforceability solely on the grounds that it is in the data message purporting to give rise to such legal effect, or that it is merely referred to in that electronic data message.

Section 7
Legal Recognition of Electronic Documents

Electronic documents shall have the legal effect, validity or enforceability as any other document or legal writing, and—

(a) Where the law requires a document to be in writing, that requirement is met by an electronic document if the said electronic document maintains its integrity and reliability and can be authenticated so as to be usable for subsequent reference.

(b) Where the law requires that a document be presented or retained in its original form, that requirement is met by an electronic document if there exists a reliable assurance as to the integrity of the document from the time when it was first generated in its final form.

Section 8
Legal Recognition of Electronic Signatures

An electronic signature on the electronic document shall be equivalent to the signature of a person on a written document if that signature is proved by showing that a prescribed procedure, not alterable by the parties interested in the electronic document, existed under which—

(a) A method is used to identify the party sought to be bound and to indicate said party's access to the electronic document necessary for his consent or approval through the electronic signature;

(b) Said method is reliable and appropriate for the purpose for which the electronic document was generated or communicated, in the light of all circumstances, including any relevant agreement.

Section 16
Formation and Validity of Electronic Contracts

Except as otherwise agreed by the parties, an offer or the acceptance of an offer may be expressed in, conveyed or made by means of electronic data message or electronic document. No contract shall be denied validity or enforceability solely on the grounds that it is constituted through the use of electronic means.

Sections 30–37
Hacking, Cracking and Penal Provisions

Hacking or Cracking (Sec. 33) — Unauthorized access into or interference in a computer system/server/information and communication system; or any access in order to corrupt, alter, steal, or destroy using a computer or other similar information and communication devices, without the knowledge and consent of the owner of the computer or information and communications system, including the introduction of computer viruses and the like, resulting in the corruption, destruction, alteration, theft or loss of electronic data messages or electronic document shall be punished by a minimum fine of ₱100,000 and a maximum commensurate to the damage incurred and a mandatory imprisonment of six (6) months to three (3) years.

Piracy (Sec. 33) — The unauthorized copying, reproduction, dissemination, distribution, importation, use, removal, alteration, substitution, modification, storage, uploading, downloading, communication, making available to the public, or broadcasting of protected material shall be punished by a minimum fine of ₱100,000 and a maximum commensurate to the damage incurred and a mandatory imprisonment of six (6) months to three (3) years.

R.A. 10175 — Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012
Cybercrime Prevention Act (Related Legislation)

Supplements the E-Commerce Act by penalizing cyber offenses including illegal access, data interference, system interference, misuse of devices, cybersquatting, computer-related fraud, computer-related identity theft, and cybersex. Strengthens enforcement against electronic crimes originally addressed in Secs. 30–33 of R.A. 8792.

R.A. 11032 — EODB Act (Indirect Impact)
Ease of Doing Business Act

Directly impacts the E-Commerce Act by mandating automation of government transactions and services, requiring government agencies to establish online platforms for applications, payments, and processing — reinforcing the legal basis of electronic transactions under R.A. 8792.

IRR of R.A. 8792
Implementing Rules and Regulations

Issued jointly by DICT, DTI, DOF, and other agencies. Sets out standards for electronic document management, digital signature authentication, and electronic notarization. Mandates the use of Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) for government electronic transactions.

Summary & Key Takeaways
R.A. 8792
Core Purpose: Grants legal recognition to electronic documents, data messages, and electronic signatures, thereby enabling electronic commerce and transactions to have the same validity as traditional written instruments.
01
Electronic Equivalence Doctrine — Electronic data messages and documents are legally equivalent to paper documents. No document can be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic.
02
Electronic Signature — An electronic signature on a document is equivalent to a handwritten signature, provided the authentication method used is reliable and the signatory's identity is verifiable.
03
Electronic Contracts (Sec. 16) — Contracts formed through electronic means are valid and enforceable. Acceptance may be expressed through electronic data messages.
04
Consumer Protection (Sec. 20) — Prescribes minimum disclosures for e-commerce transactions: identity of seller, terms and conditions, price, method of payment, delivery, and right of withdrawal.
05
Hacking and Piracy Penalties — Unauthorized access, interference, or piracy carries minimum fine of ₱100,000 and imprisonment of 6 months to 3 years (now supplemented by R.A. 10175).
06
Government Use (Sec. 27) — Government agencies are authorized to use electronic documents and data messages in communications, transactions, proceedings, and record-keeping.

Ease of Doing Business Act
Republic Act No. 11032 — Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018
Full Text
Summary & Takeaways
Reviewer
Ask AI
✦ ✦ ✦
Section 1
Title

This Act shall be known as the "Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018."

Section 2
Declaration of Policy

It is hereby declared the policy of the State to promote integrity, accountability, proper management of public affairs and public property as well as to establish effective practices aimed at the prevention of graft and corruption in government. Towards this end, the State shall maintain honesty and responsibility among its public officials and employees, and shall take appropriate measures to promote transparency in each agency with regard to the manner of transacting with the public, which shall encompass a program for the adoption of simplified requirements and procedures that will reduce red tape and expedite business and nonbusiness related transactions in government.

Section 9
Citizen's Charter

All government agencies including local government units, government-owned and -controlled corporations, and other government instrumentalities, whether located in the Philippines or abroad, shall set up their respective service standards to be known as the Citizen's Charter in the form of information billboards which shall be posted at the main entrance of offices or at the most conspicuous place, and in their respective websites.

The Citizen's Charter shall contain the following information: (a) identification of the frontline service; (b) the step-by-step procedure to obtain a particular service; (c) the name of the responsible officer or employee at each step; (d) the maximum time to conclude the process; (e) the document/s to be presented by the client; (f) the amount of fees, if necessary; (g) the procedure for filing complaints.

Section 10
Reengineering of Systems and Procedures

All offices and agencies which provide government services shall be required to regularly examine and streamline policies, rules and regulations, and procedures in accordance with the mandate of R.A. 11032. The Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA) shall oversee this reengineering process.

Prescribed Processing Times:

— Simple transactions: 3 working days

— Complex transactions: 7 working days

— Highly technical applications: 20 working days

Section 16
Zero-Contact Policy

Except during the preliminary assessment of the request and evaluation of sufficiency of submitted requirements, no government officer or employee shall have any contact, in any manner, unless strictly necessary, with any applicant or requesting party concerning an application or request. As such, any government officer or employee who is found to be requesting, soliciting or accepting, directly or indirectly, any gift, gratuity, favor, entertainment, loan or anything of monetary value shall be punished in accordance with the Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act and other applicable laws.

Section 17
Automatic Extension of Business Permits

Business permits shall be automatically extended without any condition imposed, pending the completion of any required evaluation or assessment, when the concerned government agency fails to act on an application within the prescribed processing period. The same rule shall apply to the issuance of local business licenses, clearances, permits, certifications or authorizations, unless otherwise provided by applicable laws and regulations.

Summary & Key Takeaways
R.A. 11032
Core Purpose: Reduces red tape, simplifies government procedures, and mandates efficient delivery of government services to businesses and citizens.
01
Processing Time Limits — Simple: 3 working days; Complex: 7 working days; Highly Technical: 20 working days. Failure to act → automatic approval (for business permits).
02
One-Stop Shop — Mandates the establishment of Business One-Stop Shops (BOSS) in all LGUs for business permit applications and renewals.
03
Anti-Red Tape Authority (ARTA) — Created to implement and oversee compliance, investigate complaints, impose sanctions, and coordinate with all government agencies.
04
Zero-Contact Policy (Sec. 16) — Government officers cannot have unnecessary contact with applicants. Violations subject to criminal and administrative liability.
05
Citizen's Charter — Required posting of service standards including step-by-step procedures, responsible officers, timeframes, fees, and complaint procedures.
06
Penalties for Non-Compliance — Administrative liability: suspension 1–30 days (1st), 1–3 months (2nd), dismissal with forfeiture of benefits (3rd). Criminal: imprisonment 1–6 years and/or fine of ₱500,000–₱2,000,000.

Labor Code of the Philippines
Presidential Decree No. 442 — As Amended by R.A. 10151, R.A. 10361, R.A. 10396, and Other Laws
Full Text
Amendments
Summary & Takeaways
Reviewer
Ask AI
✦ ✦ ✦
Article 1
Name of Decree

This Decree shall be known as the "Labor Code of the Philippines."

Article 2
Date of Effectivity

This Code shall take effect six (6) months after its promulgation.

Article 3
Declaration of Basic Policy

The State shall afford protection to labor, promote full employment, ensure equal work opportunities regardless of sex, race or creed and regulate the relations between workers and employers. The State shall assure the rights of workers to self-organization, collective bargaining, security of tenure, and just and humane conditions of work.

Article 83
Normal Hours of Work

The normal hours of work of any employee shall not exceed eight (8) hours a day. Health personnel in cities and municipalities with a population of at least one million (1,000,000) or in hospitals and clinics with a bed capacity of at least one hundred (100) shall hold regular office hours for eight (8) hours a day, for five (5) days a week, exclusive of time for meals, except where the exigencies of the service require that such personnel work for six (6) days or forty-eight (48) hours, in which case, they shall be entitled to an additional compensation of at least thirty percent (30%) of their regular wage for work on the sixth day.

Article 87
Overtime Work

Work may be performed beyond eight (8) hours a day provided that the employee is paid for the overtime work, an additional compensation equivalent to his regular wage plus at least twenty-five percent (25%) thereof. Work performed beyond eight hours on a holiday or rest day shall be paid an additional compensation equivalent to the rate of the first eight hours on a holiday or rest day plus at least thirty percent (30%) thereof.

Article 94
Right to Holiday Pay

Every worker shall be paid his regular daily wage during regular holidays, except in retail and service establishments regularly employing less than ten (10) workers.

Article 279 (As Renumbered — Article 294)
Security of Tenure

In cases of regular employment, the employer shall not terminate the services of an employee except for a just or authorized cause and only after due process. An employee who is unjustly dismissed from work shall be entitled to reinstatement without loss of seniority rights and other privileges and to his full backwages, inclusive of allowances, and to his other benefits or their monetary equivalent computed from the time his compensation was withheld from him up to the time of his actual reinstatement.

Article 282 (As Renumbered — Article 297)
Termination by Employer — Just Causes

An employer may terminate an employment for any of the following causes:

(a) Serious misconduct or willful disobedience by the employee of the lawful orders of his employer or representative in connection with his work;

(b) Gross and habitual neglect by the employee of his duties;

(c) Fraud or willful breach by the employee of the trust reposed in him by his employer;

(d) Commission of a crime or offense by the employee against the person of his employer or any immediate member of his family or his duly authorized representative;

(e) Other causes analogous to the foregoing.

Article 283 (As Renumbered — Article 298)
Termination by Employer — Authorized Causes

An employer may terminate employment of any employee due to installation of labor-saving devices, redundancy, retrenchment to prevent losses or the closing or cessation of operation of the establishment or undertaking. In case of retrenchment: at least one (1) month advance notice plus separation pay equivalent to at least one (1) month pay for every year of service.

R.A. 10151 — Night Shift Work
Night Shift Workers Act

Amended Articles 86 and 87 to explicitly cover night shift workers and align Philippine labor law with international labor standards. Night shift differential pay (10% of regular wage per hour worked between 10PM–6AM) was codified.

R.A. 10361 — Kasambahay Law
Domestic Workers Act

Established the rights and benefits of domestic workers (kasambahay), including minimum wage (NCR: ₱6,500/month), paid vacation and sick leave, SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG coverage, and protection from abuse. Supplemented the Labor Code's provisions on household service workers.

R.A. 11058 — OSHS Act
Occupational Safety and Health Standards Act

Strengthened occupational safety and health standards. Requires employers to provide PPE, safety officers, and safe working conditions. Mandates workers' right to refuse unsafe work without loss of pay. Penalties: ₱100,000 per day of non-compliance.

R.A. 11165 — Telecommuting
Telecommuting Act

Authorizes employers in the private sector to offer telecommuting programs. Telecommuting employees enjoy same rights and benefits as regular employees, including minimum wage, overtime, and social security coverage. Telecommuting arrangement must be voluntary and documented.

Summary & Key Takeaways
P.D. 442
Core Purpose: Establishes the rights, duties, and obligations of employers and employees, regulates working conditions, and provides mechanisms for settlement of labor disputes.
01
Security of Tenure (Art. 294) — Regular employees may only be dismissed for just or authorized causes and only after due process (twin notice rule: (1) show-cause notice; (2) notice of termination).
02
Five Just Causes (Art. 297) — Serious misconduct, gross neglect, fraud/breach of trust, commission of a crime, and analogous causes.
03
Authorized Causes (Art. 298–299) — Labor-saving devices, redundancy, retrenchment, disease, and closure of business. Requires 30-day notice to DOLE and employee, plus separation pay.
04
Hours of Work — Normal: 8 hours/day. Overtime: +25% of regular rate. Night shift: +10%. Rest day/holiday: +30% of first 8 hours rate.
05
13th Month Pay (P.D. 851) — Mandatory for all employees receiving ₱1,000 or less per month (now effectively all rank-and-file employees). Equal to 1/12 of total basic salary within the calendar year.
06
Labor Relations — Workers have the right to self-organization, collective bargaining, and concerted activities. Unfair labor practices by employers and labor organizations are prohibited.
Quick Reviewer — Labor Code
Situational — Question 1
Maria works as a regular employee at XYZ Corp. She was dismissed because the company decided to automate its manufacturing line, eliminating her position. She received no advance notice and no separation pay. What are her legal remedies?
A
File for illegal dismissal only — there is no authorized cause here
B
The dismissal was valid; no remedy available as business decisions are the employer's prerogative
C
File for illegal dismissal due to failure of due process and non-payment of separation pay; claim reinstatement or backwages and separation pay
D
File a complaint for retrenchment without just cause
Correct. While automation (labor-saving device) is an authorized cause under Art. 298, proper procedure requires: (1) 30-day written notice to DOLE and employee; (2) separation pay of 1 month pay per year of service. Failure to comply renders the dismissal illegal as to procedure, entitling Maria to nominal damages and full separation pay.

Social Security Law of 2018
Republic Act No. 11199 — Social Security Act of 2018
Full Text
Summary & Takeaways
Reviewer
Ask AI
✦ ✦ ✦
Section 1
Title

This Act shall be known as the "Social Security Act of 2018."

Section 2
Declaration of Policy

It is the policy of the Republic of the Philippines to establish, develop, promote and perfect a sound and viable tax-exempt social security system suitable to the needs of the people throughout the Philippines which shall promote social justice and provide meaningful protection to members and their beneficiaries against the hazards of disability, sickness, maternity, old age, death, and other contingencies resulting in loss of income or financial burden. Towards this end, the State shall endeavor to extend social security protection to every Filipino worker.

Section 8 — Key Definitions
Definitions

(d) Employer — Any person, natural or juridical, domestic or foreign, who carries on in the Philippines any trade, business, industry, undertaking or activity of any kind and uses the services of another person who is under his orders as regards the employment.

(e) Monthly salary credit (MSC) — The compensation base for contributions and benefits related to the total earnings for the month.

(f) Monthly pension — The basic amount received by a retiree or a beneficiary upon the member's retirement, death, or disability.

Section 9
Coverage

Compulsory Coverage: Coverage in the SSS shall be compulsory upon all employees not over sixty (60) years of age and their employers. Household helpers, self-employed persons, overseas Filipino workers, and non-working spouses may also be covered.

Voluntary Coverage: Coverage in the SSS shall also be extended on a voluntary basis to self-employed persons and OFWs upon their registration with the SSS.

Section 11
Contributions

The monthly contributions shall be based on the monthly salary credit (MSC). Effective 2023: Employee share = 4.5%; Employer share = 9.5%; Total = 14%. The contribution rate shall increase annually until it reaches the final rate prescribed by the actuarial study.

Minimum MSC: ₱4,000 | Maximum MSC: ₱30,000 (as of 2024 schedule).

Section 12
Monthly Pension

The monthly pension shall be the highest of:

(a) Three hundred pesos (₱300) plus twenty percent (20%) of the average monthly salary credit plus two percent (2%) of the average monthly salary credit for each credited year of service in excess of ten (10) years; or

(b) Forty percent (40%) of the average monthly salary credit; or

(c) One thousand pesos (₱1,000) per month.

Sections 13–16
Benefits

Retirement Benefit (Sec. 12) — Member who is at least 60 (optional) or 65 (compulsory) years old and has paid at least 120 monthly contributions.

Death Benefit (Sec. 13) — Monthly pension to primary beneficiaries, or lump-sum to secondary beneficiaries.

Disability Benefit (Sec. 13-A) — Monthly disability pension or lump-sum for partial/total, temporary/permanent disability.

Sickness Benefit (Sec. 14) — Daily allowance for members unable to work due to illness or injury. 90% of average daily salary credit for up to 120 days per year.

Maternity Benefit (Sec. 14-A) — 100% of average daily salary credit (previously 60 days normal delivery, now extended to 105 days under R.A. 11210).

Summary & Key Takeaways
R.A. 11199
Core Purpose: Provides social protection to Filipino workers against hazards of disability, sickness, maternity, old age, and death through a contributory insurance system.
01
Coverage — Compulsory for all employees (below 60), employers, kasambahay, and OFWs. Voluntary for self-employed persons and non-working spouses.
02
Increased Contributions — R.A. 11199 increased contribution rates progressively. 2024 rate: 14% total (4.5% employee + 9.5% employer). MSC range: ₱4,000–₱30,000.
03
Retirement Conditions — Minimum 120 monthly contributions; age 60 (optional retirement) or 65 (mandatory). Monthly pension is the highest of three formulas.
04
Key Benefits — Retirement, death, disability, sickness, maternity, funeral, and unemployment insurance benefits. R.A. 11199 added Unemployment Insurance/Involuntary Separation Benefit.
05
Unemployment Benefit (New) — SSS members involuntarily separated may receive 50% of average monthly salary credit for up to 2 months, provided they have at least 36 monthly contributions within the last 18 months.
06
Penalties for Non-Remittance — Employer who fails to remit contributions: imprisonment of 6 years + 1 day to 12 years and fine not less than ₱5,000 nor more than ₱20,000 per employee affected.
